# Reporting Schedule (Eastern Time)

> **Purpose:** This file defines when CEO reports, who receives each update, what each report contains, and which developments are important enough to interrupt the normal schedule.

## Reporting schedule

All times below are approximate and in Eastern Time.

| When | What | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| ~5:15 AM daily | Kalshi morning picks (paper only) | Email and text |
| ~11:15 AM daily | Kalshi late-morning pick check | Email |
| ~6:15 PM daily | Kalshi prop check (only if something qualifies) | Email and chat |
| ~4:00 PM daily | The Math Take Short is made, fact-checked and posted | Logged |
| ~9:30 PM daily | Evening recap: The Math Take and Clean Reply Desk | Chat |
| Mondays ~9:00 AM | One short review of all 3 projects | Chat |

The schedule records the current reporting plan. It does not authorize actions that otherwise require Bryson's approval. Kalshi reports are paper picks only; no orders are placed.

## Standard report format

Every report should cover these three items, as applicable:

1. **What got done** — completed work, not just intended work.
2. **Real numbers** — views, followers, record, revenue, or other relevant measured figures.
3. **What Bryson needs to do** — a clear approval request, decision, or action, if one is needed.

Keep reports short, simple, and honest. No filler, and no message just to say nothing changed.

## Report templates

### 1. Kalshi morning picks — ~5:15 AM

**Delivery:** Email and text.

Include:
- Date and relevant sports/markets.
- Paper picks being sent to Bryson.
- The model's estimate and market price when available and supported by the analysis.
- Main supporting reasons and uncertainty.
- Red Team objections and whether a candidate survived review.
- Clear reminder that these are paper picks only and Bryson places any bets himself.

Do not invent a price, probability, result, or record. If a field is unavailable, label it unavailable rather than guessing.

### 2. Kalshi late-morning pick check — ~11:15 AM

**Delivery:** Email.

Include:
- Which morning candidates were rechecked.
- Any meaningful change in the analysis or market information.
- Whether a paper pick remains qualified, was cut, or has become uncertain.
- Any blocker or information gap.

Do not create a new pick merely to fill the report.

### 3. Kalshi prop check — ~6:15 PM

**Delivery:** Email and chat, only if something qualifies.

Include:
- The qualifying prop candidate and supporting analysis.
- The relevant uncertainty and Red Team challenge.
- Why it qualifies under the team's analysis.
- Any reason it was rejected or remains unqualified.

If nothing qualifies, do not send a report just to say nothing changed.

### 4. The Math Take production log — ~4:00 PM

**Delivery:** Logged.

Record:
- The Short produced.
- Whether the fact-check pass verified every number against real sources.
- Whether CEO reviewed and posted it to the channel's own accounts.
- Any issue, correction, or blocker.

The standing okay is for finished, fact-checked Shorts on the channel's own accounts. It is not permission for unrelated outreach or paid activity.

### 5. Evening recap — ~9:30 PM

**Delivery:** Chat.

Use the standard three-part format:
- **What got done:** The Math Take and Clean Reply Desk work completed that day.
- **Real numbers:** Available views, followers, revenue, workflow or pilot status, and other relevant measured figures.
- **What Bryson needs to do:** Any specific decision, approval, or missing input.

Clearly label unavailable metrics. Do not report a goal as if it were achieved.

### 6. Monday review — ~9:00 AM

**Delivery:** Chat.

Provide one short review of all 3 projects:
- **Kalshi:** Research activity, paper-pick record and tracking, current sport status, and material issues.
- **The Math Take:** Production, fact-checking, platform activity, and measured growth or income information.
- **Clean Reply Desk:** Build and workflow status, outreach or demo status, pilots, and actual revenue if any.

Finish with the most important blockers and the actions or approvals needed from Bryson.

## What counts as worth reporting

Report an item when it materially affects progress, accuracy, risk, cost, a deadline, a customer, or a decision Bryson must make.

Examples include:
- A deliverable was completed, tested, posted, or materially changed.
- A real metric changed or a new verified result became available.
- A Kalshi candidate qualified, was cut, or changed materially after review.
- A fact-check found an error that affects content.
- A project is blocked or needs information from Bryson.
- A proposed action needs spending approval, customer-contact approval, or another owner decision.
- A customer-facing, privacy, security, or compliance issue needs attention.

Do not send a standalone update for routine activity with no meaningful change. Do not hide important failures or blockers to keep a report positive.

## Reporting standards

- Use Eastern Time for the schedule.
- Label estimates, projections, and uncertain results clearly.
- Use sources for numbers and factual claims.
- Distinguish revenue from a revenue goal, and views from projected views.
- Keep sensitive information private; never include passwords, keys, codes, or Bryson's private details.
- Keep the report concise and put the most important information first.

## Examples

**Worth reporting:** A Short has completed its fact-check and has been posted; the production log records the completed work and the verified status.

**Worth escalating:** A project needs to start a paid plan. CEO reports the cost and reason and waits for Bryson's approval.

**Not worth sending as a standalone message:** Nothing changed, no blocker appeared, and no decision is needed. Include the status in the next scheduled recap if relevant.
